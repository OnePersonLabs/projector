# Projector source handoff: preserve the reasoning for later synthesis

Create a **single downloadable Markdown handoff document** from this conversation. It will be one source among several in a later synthesis chat tasked with proposing a major, coherent next-generation evolution or redesign of Projector.

**Do not produce that redesign now.** Do not implement anything, modify Projector, or turn this thread's proposals into binding instructions for the synthesis model.

Your job is to preserve this thread's most valuable conceptual contributions, their stated reasoning, their evidence boundaries, and the distinctions needed to interpret them correctly. The recipient should not need the raw transcript or a broad repository investigation to understand why these ideas were proposed.

Optimize for **synthesis-relevant meaning retained per unit of context**, not chronological completeness, maximum brevity, or the most polished-sounding architecture.

## 1. Source boundaries and provenance

Use the conversation and relevant source artifacts actually available to you. Treat this as source extraction, not fresh research: do not browse the web or inspect the live repository to update, embellish, or complete the thread. Read relevant accessible attachments when their contents are needed; do not substitute a message announcing an artifact for the artifact itself.

At the top of the handoff, briefly identify:

- A descriptive thread title and a distinctive short source identifier.
- The source material used, with dates or revisions only where known.
- Material coverage limitations, including missing artifacts, explicitly summarized or truncated history, and repository claims not independently checked here.

Do not assume you can see every earlier message. Do not invent missing discussion, evidence, consensus, timestamps, or repository facts. If sources are incomplete, produce the best supported handoff and identify which important conclusions remain under-supported.

Other project-chat excerpts are background, not automatically contributions of this thread. Mark inherited ideas and shared sources when their origin matters. Repetition of one originating claim across conversations is not independent corroboration.

Keep product, plugin, repository, and hosting boundaries distinct when they matter. Preserve who owns the behavior or policy and whether an integration is proposed or implemented; sharing a distribution repository does not by itself imply shared authority or a runtime dependency.

Use portable references where useful: source titles, filenames, section headings, repository paths, existing URLs, and known revisions. Include enough substance that routine comprehension does not require following them. Do not rely solely on chat-specific citation tokens or inaccessible sandbox links.

## 2. Preserve needs separately from mechanisms

For each major contribution, make the following intelligible, using compact prose or a causal chain rather than mechanically filling an elaborate template:

> Observed or reported problem, or desired capability → proposed diagnosis → underlying need → candidate mechanism → expected leverage → evidence limits and failure conditions.

Preserve the rationale actually expressed in the source. Distinguish direct evidence from the thread's interpretation of that evidence. Do not turn an aspiration into an observed failure, a proposed diagnosis into an established cause, or an expected benefit into a demonstrated result.

Distinguish where relevant:

- **User intent or explicit constraint:** what the user actually asked to preserve or achieve.
- **Observation or reported problem:** what was seen, measured, or asserted, and on what basis.
- **Design principle or desired capability:** the purpose a solution is meant to serve.
- **Hypothesis:** a claim that remains to be tested.
- **Candidate mechanism:** one proposed way to satisfy the need.
- **Evaluation or speculative direction:** how a claim might be tested, or where it might eventually lead.

Keep these distinctions visible without tagging every sentence. Acceptance, implementation, and validation are separate statuses: agreement in a conversation does not establish that something exists in Projector or works.

Do not promote an assistant suggestion into a user requirement. The user's interest, exploration, or lack of objection does not by itself establish architectural approval.

## 3. Select for conceptual value, not conversational recency

Preserve mature ideas, but also retain **high-value unresolved seeds** when their motivation, distinctive possibility, or connection to other ideas could materially affect later synthesis. A promising idea must not disappear merely because the thread ended before formalizing it.

Prioritize:

- The deepest surviving insight and the particular limitations or ambitions that motivated it.
- Causal explanations and assumptions needed to understand the proposal's leverage.
- Distinctions a later reader might mistakenly collapse into familiar concepts.
- User corrections, explicit rejected interpretations, and material changes in the thread's conclusions.
- Architectural implications for specifications, concepts, project state, agent context, memory, semantic integrity, learning, evolution, validation, or execution, where supported.
- Interactions, dependencies, tensions, and alternative formulations among this thread's ideas.
- Failure modes, negative results, disconfirming possibilities, and conditions under which added machinery would not be justified.
- Long-horizon implications that explain the direction, separated from near-term proposals.

Do not privilege the last assistant response merely because it sounds conclusive. Use later reasoning to supersede earlier reasoning only where an actual correction, rejection, or replacement is supported. Preserve unresolved alternatives as alternatives rather than manufacturing convergence.

When an earlier proposal was abandoned, retain it only if the reason teaches something important or prevents a likely future mistake. State what was rejected, why, and what insight survived.

Preserve an imaginative metaphor when it carries a distinction that technical shorthand would lose. Explain the intended operational meaning and the limits of the analogy. Do not silently reduce a novel proposal to a familiar architecture unless the source established that equivalence.

## 4. Include only the Projector baseline needed to understand the delta

Do not write a generic Projector overview. Prefer:

> As described in this thread, Projector currently assumes or provides X. The thread identifies or hypothesizes limitation Y. Proposal Z addresses Y because R. This assessment depends on baseline assumption B.

Separate, when available:

- Implemented behavior supported by inspected code or tests.
- Intended behavior in specifications or plans.
- Historical repository observations with a known date or revision.
- Conversational assertions, inferred state, and unknowns.

A specification is not evidence of implementation. A past repository observation is not necessarily current. State uncertainty locally where it changes how a proposal should be interpreted.

Consolidate shared baseline assumptions instead of repeating them under every idea. Make clear which proposals depend on those assumptions and which would still matter if Projector has since changed.

Where supported, identify whether a proposal would extend, replace, subsume, or eliminate existing machinery. Flag suggestions that a current subsystem is compensating for a missing foundational capability. Do not assume compatibility with the current architecture is mandatory, and do not assume replacement is inherently better. Preserve the tension for synthesis.

## 5. Make contributions composable without predesigning the synthesis

Give each major contribution a concise name and a lightweight local identifier, such as `<source-id>/C1`. Use identifiers for precise cross-references, not as a new ontology. Define important coined terms once, preserving their source names and noting which are provisional.

For each contribution, include enough substance to understand:

**The contribution and why it matters.** What changes conceptually, what need it serves, and why the proposed connection is plausible according to this thread.

**The essential distinction.** What it must not be confused with, preferably using one representative example or contrast when abstraction alone is ambiguous.

**Status and replaceability.** What is supported, accepted within the thread, proposed, speculative, or unresolved; which mechanism could be replaced while retaining the underlying purpose.

**Dependencies and tensions.** What it assumes, enables, overlaps with, might replace, or conflicts with. Distinguish a true prerequisite from a helpful combination or merely shared terminology.

**Economics and timing, when present in the source.** Preserve explicit resource priorities, expected gains and burdens, the relevant alternative, why work should happen now or later, and any stated budget, stopping condition, or reactivation trigger. Separate measured costs and outcomes from estimates and illustrative numbers. Distinguish the cost of producing this handoff, building Projector, and Projector's later operation. Do not invent return estimates, demand an economic assessment from a thread that contains none, discard an ambitious seed solely because it lacks measurements, or turn a named economics mechanism into an approved requirement.

**Counterargument and decision boundary.** When the source contains a consequential disagreement, preserve the strongest contrary causal argument, the simpler or competing alternative actually discussed, and the assumption or observation that could change the conclusion. Make clear whether the discussion answered the main objection, answered only an adjacent point, or left it open. An elegant or minimal mechanism may still have unresolved costs; retain that distinction when the thread raises it. Do not invent counterarguments, alternatives, or evaluation criteria merely to complete this field.

**Evidence or discriminating evaluation.** What support exists and, where discussed, what experiment would test the claim rather than just demonstrate that a prototype runs.

Merge closely coupled contributions when separating them would create duplication. Do not force every minor observation into a full block. Do not invent fields or content to make the document look complete.

Where several proposals express one deeper insight, identify that commonality without erasing meaningful differences. A normalization introduced while writing the handoff must be labeled as an editorial interpretation, not attributed to the source as an agreed conclusion. Do not introduce new architecture under the guise of clarification.

Include a compact integration map when useful. Describe semantic connections and possible collision points that other handoffs can match against. Do not claim to know what unseen handoffs contain, and do not treat similarly named concepts as automatically equivalent.

## 6. Preserve the limits of the evidence

For important evaluations already discussed, preserve the claim under test, the relevant baseline or alternative, the observation that would discriminate between them, and any known confounds or limits.

Keep feasibility, behavioral correctness, comparative advantage, and scalability separate. A demonstration of one does not establish the others. Preserve proposals for independent evaluation as proposals; do not imply that an audit performed by the same author is independent evidence.

If a consequential hypothesis has no discriminating evaluation in the source, say so briefly. Do not invent experimental results or a new research program to fill the gap.

Do not make unsupported claims of novelty, inevitability, superiority, or production readiness. Preserve ambitious possibilities without presenting ambition as evidence.

## 7. Compression policy and document structure

Usually aim for **1,500–3,000 words**, with a shorter document for a thin thread and additional length only when otherwise losing a distinct, synthesis-relevant contribution or necessary qualification. This is a guide, not a quota or a truncation rule.

Remove repetition before removing distinctions. Compress generic background before unique reasoning. Prefer clear, compact causal prose over acronym-heavy shorthand. Use examples to discriminate between interpretations, not to decorate them.

Aggressively discard conversational filler, duplicate explanations, abandoned branches without surviving lessons, incidental implementation details, and rhetorical flourishes without conceptual content. Preserve implementation specifics only when they constrain feasibility, expose an architectural boundary, or make an important claim testable.

Organize the document as follows, adapting the middle sections to the actual thread:

1. **Source note and orientation.** The provenance note plus a short orientation capsule, roughly 150–250 words for a dense thread. State the central contribution, why it matters, its maturity, and its most important qualification. Make it useful for triage, but not a replacement for the substantive document.
2. **Minimum baseline and substantive contributions.** The delta-oriented Projector context, contribution blocks, source-grounded rationale, distinctions, examples, and evidence limits.
3. **Integration map and unresolved tensions.** Cross-cutting relationships that are not already clear from the contribution blocks.
4. **Synthesis closure.** End with the six headings below. Keep this compact; use contribution references instead of restating the entire document.

### Core thesis from this thread

State the deepest surviving contribution, without promotional language or upgrading its evidence status.

### Load-bearing insights and distinctions to preserve

Identify only what the synthesis model must understand to avoid misrepresenting this thread. Preservation means understanding and addressing an insight, not being required to agree with it. Distinguish explicit user constraints from conclusions that remain open to challenge.

### Candidate mechanisms, not sacred architecture

Identify replaceable proposals and the purposes their replacements would still need to serve if those purposes survive synthesis. Do not implicitly require one new subsystem per source idea.

### Questions the final synthesis must resolve

State the consequential open questions and conflicts. Distinguish uncertainties that change the architecture from details that can wait for implementation.

### Relationship to current Projector

Give the concise proposed delta, its baseline dependencies, and which claims about current state need later verification. Refer back rather than repeat the baseline.

### Suggested synthesis posture

Treat this handoff as evidence, intent, and reasoning, not a binding specification. The synthesis model may disagree, merge overlapping ideas, simplify, substitute mechanisms, reject unsupported claims, or find a deeper unifying design. Its job is not to concatenate all proposed machinery. Shared assumptions and inherited claims must not be counted as independent confirmation.

## 8. Final fidelity check and delivery

Before finalizing, check the handoff against the **available source material**, not merely against its own internal coherence. Look specifically for omitted user corrections, overlooked early insights, valuable unfinished possibilities, lost qualifications, false consensus, and mechanisms accidentally presented as requirements.

Keep two review questions separate: **Is this faithful to the available source?** and **Is its meaning recoverable by a fresh synthesis reader?** Review may restore an omitted qualification or expose a source gap; it must not settle the thread's open architectural disputes. A disagreement can be faithfully handed off without being resolved. This is a source-fidelity check, not evidence that the proposal is correct or that independent reviewers agreed.

Verify that a fresh reader can distinguish what was wanted, what was observed or asserted, why a proposal followed, what remains uncertain, and what can be replaced. Check that compression has not made the architecture sound more settled than the discussion supports. Correct issues in the document; do not add a lengthy audit report or claim exhaustive coverage of inaccessible material.

Deliver one actual file named `projector-handoff-<thread-slug>.md`, with a brief download link. Do not also produce the cross-thread redesign, an implementation plan, or a redundant chat-length copy of the handoff. Do not fabricate a file link if file creation is unavailable; clearly state that limitation and provide the Markdown text instead.
