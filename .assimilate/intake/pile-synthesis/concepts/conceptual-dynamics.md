# Conceptual dynamics

`§conceptual-dynamics` · What should change, what should remain, and why.

## Core

A concept is usefully modeled as a **relational pattern with characteristic behavior under transformations**, not only a label attached to a list of properties. Meaning depends on distinctions, relationships, context, and consequences.

“Stretchability,” for example, is not exhausted by a field stored on an isolated triangle. It depends on the transformation, geometry, dimension, material assumptions, and question being asked. The engineering lesson is not that nodes, graphs, or vectors are forbidden. It is that a chosen representation must preserve the distinctions the task depends on.

## Three kinds of transformation

| Kind | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Surface variation | Ignore differences that do not change the relevant meaning. |
| Structured semantic transformation | Track how consequences change with the transformation. |
| Authorized revision of purpose or rules | Update the governing conception and affected conclusions. |

These correspond loosely to invariance, equivariance, and intentional changes to the governing rules. The terms are useful only when the preserved and changed properties are specified.

A model that rejects every change is not faithful. A model that treats every paraphrase as a new concept is not stable. A model that accepts every near-synonym as equivalent misses meaningful differences.

## Identity is contextual, not arbitrary

Stable concept handles allow references to survive changing descriptions and representations. They do not prove a concept has an independent location in a neural model or a permanent boundary in the architecture.

Renaming an internal variable can be incidental. Renaming a public field can break consumers. Therefore, “renames are unimportant” is not a valid universal rule. The consequence depends on the applicable contracts and external obligations.

The same caution applies to conceptual merging. Authentication identity and display identity may once have shared a name while serving different responsibilities. A clean synthesis preserves the distinction when it changes behavior; it does not collapse both into a tidier paragraph.

## A useful operational test

Apply a small, controlled change and ask which consequences should move.

For an illustrative system, allowing offline sessions to certify canonical skill may affect assessment authority, evidence trust, deduplication, and synchronization policy. It need not change audio-clock ownership or rendering architecture. The important test is whether the model identifies both the affected and unaffected regions without being told the answer.

The analogy to a derivative is helpful: sensitivity of project consequences to a change in premises. No claim of a differentiable, complete semantic simulator is required.

Test the reverse direction too. Preserve every familiar term but change who has authority to certify an outcome. A representation focused on wording may miss the change; one tracking responsibilities should detect it.

## Preservation includes generativity

The target is not a museum of constraints. Understanding should support new coherent possibilities, including designs not stated verbatim in the training material or specification.

A useful candidate can improve one dimension while damaging another. Such gain-with-loss pairs reveal tradeoffs more effectively than generic “is this good?” questions. A better candidate may preserve both gains, or reveal that an assumed tradeoff was unnecessary.

Negative space matters: why an attractive alternative fails under current conditions, and which premise would reopen it. Preserve conditional reasons, not permanent prohibitions derived from temporary circumstances.

## Projection limits

A task-specific view is intentionally partial. It need not uniquely reconstruct the entire conception. Requiring a perfect round trip from every small projection would defeat progressive disclosure.

Instead, require fidelity for the view's disclosed scope, visibility of consequential unknowns, and consistency where views overlap. A recipient should know when its current perspective is insufficient.

Agreement among projections is not independent proof of correctness. Multiple views generated from one mistaken premise can be mutually consistent. External observations and independently established obligations must be able to challenge them.

## Representation remains an experimental choice

Hypergraphs, vector geometry, learned models, structured rules, and hybrids are candidate tools. “Neural” does not automatically mean holistic; “symbolic” does not automatically mean lossy. Compare what each can preserve, infer, revise, and verify at acceptable cost.
