# Learning curriculum

`§learning-curriculum` · Preserve the experiences needed to learn a conception, not just a compressed list of facts.

## Core

A reconciled current conception is useful, but it should not become the only training material. Otherwise, rich experience is flattened into a chosen prose or graph schema and the learner must reconstruct everything lost in that serialization.

The curriculum should be a **learning environment** containing accepted evidence, developer distinctions, equivalent expressions, implementation manifestations, behavior traces, successful and failed transformations, counterfactuals, conditional rationale, and cross-concept situations.

Explicit anchors remain necessary where exactness matters. They complement rather than replace the experience from which project-specific judgment is learned.

## Four different stores

An evidence journal preserves what was observed or decided, including provenance. The current conception states what now applies. The curriculum selects and constructs useful learning experiences. The learned checkpoint is a derived product of that curriculum.

These responsibilities need not imply four elaborate services. They do imply that a generated training example must not silently rewrite the evidence record, and that deleting an obsolete assertion from the current view need not destroy the reason it was considered.

## What survives conceptual squashing

Retain anything that affects the present conception, an unresolved choice, a live alternative, useful rationale, or the conditions under which a decision should be reopened. Remove conversational repetition and chronology that add no such information.

Three examples illustrate the distinction:

**A name correction.** An internal spelling error may be irrelevant to current reasoning. The same spelling in a public contract may create a compatibility obligation. Preserve the condition that determines whether the change matters, not a universal rule about renames.

**A rejected dependency.** An audio-library option rejected under licensing, platform, latency, or deployment constraints should carry those actual conditions and its reopening trigger. “Never use that library” is an unjustified generalization.

**A split identity concept.** If one term conflated authentication and presentation, retain the two current responsibilities and the ambiguity that caused the problem. The transcript's oscillation need not be retained in the working explanation.

## Learning meaningful differences

Useful training episodes include a harmless paraphrase, a small wording change that reverses an obligation, a structural change preserving public behavior, a new requirement that legitimately changes ownership, and a new scenario combining familiar principles in an unfamiliar way.

Gain-with-loss pairs expose the dimensions of a tradeoff. Counterfactual premises teach when an accepted answer should stop applying. Equivalence classes teach what can change without affecting a relevant obligation.

Do not optimize indiscriminate similarity between representations. Some transformations should preserve an answer; others should alter it predictably; still others should revise the rule itself. Those are different supervision targets.

## Avoid synthetic certainty

A model-generated explanation of why a decision was made is a hypothesis unless grounded in actual evidence. Smooth synthetic rationale can create a coherent fictional history and train it into the counterpart.

Label constructed scenarios and proposed implications. Keep accepted observations distinguishable from synthetic examples. Use held-out combinations, independent checks, and developer adjudication for consequential ambiguities.

A curriculum author, learner, and judge sharing the same assumptions can agree without learning the intended distinction.

## Update policy

New evidence should revise the relevant accepted state and the examples that depend on it. Preserve unaffected material. Retire or relabel superseded examples so contradictory targets do not coexist without conditions.

Keep a basis revision for generated examples and checkpoints. Evaluate changed premises and stale-checkpoint behavior explicitly. Rebuild, incremental learning, retrieval augmentation, and temporary deltas are alternatives whose costs and failure modes must be compared.

The aim is better future judgment, not a larger training dataset or a perfect-looking ontology.
