## Assistant

Yes. And that priority **strengthens** the recommendation, but changes how aggressively any `consensus-rnd` mechanism should fire.

The governing principle I’d want Projector to embody is:

> **Spend reasoning, verification, research, and agent parallelism only where their expected reduction in downstream error/rework or increase in decision quality exceeds their total burn.**

Not “do less.” Not “be thorough.” **Allocate cognition economically.**

A useful approximation is:

\[
\text{Expected Value of Work}
=
P(\text{changes outcome})
\times
\text{impact of better outcome}
+
\text{reuse/learning value}
-
\text{compute + latency + coordination cost}
\]

That should affect both **whether** Projector does something and **when** it does it.

The timing part is especially important. Expensive thinking is often best **right before a high-fanout or hard-to-reverse commitment**, but **after cheap evidence has already collapsed the search space**. Too early wastes intelligence on possibilities that disappear; too late means you're paying to discover an architectural mistake after it has propagated.

So I’d imagine Projector naturally escalating through something like:

```text
deterministic / cached answer
        ↓ if unresolved material uncertainty
cheap local analysis
        ↓
single bounded model judgment
        ↓
strong-model reasoning
        ↓
independent adversarial perspective
        ↓
multi-perspective convergence
        ↓
formal / human escalation
```

Crucially, **there is no prestige associated with reaching the bottom**. The best execution is often the one that terminates at step 1.

This makes several `consensus-rnd` ideas more attractive precisely because they can *save* quota:

- **Depth discipline**: stop reasoning once additional detail cannot change a decision.
- **Blocking authority**: prevent weak advisory concerns from spawning expensive detours.
- **Goal primacy**: don't burn quota perfecting Projector's own orchestration artifacts.
- **Focused challenge**: investigate the one unresolved causal disagreement instead of rerunning the whole panel.
- **Prospective falsifiability**: run the cheapest observation capable of settling the question.
- **Bounded convergence**: don't let disagreement turn into an infinite agent parliament.

And I would **explicitly reject fixed six-worker panels as Projector's default**. That is exactly the kind of rigid burn you don't want.

### The more interesting version

Projector could eventually learn its own **cognitive spending policy**.

Every expensive operation can leave behind evidence such as:

```text
context:
  kind of change
  affected semantic region
  uncertainty
  fanout
  reversibility
  historical failure rate

action:
  strong-model review
  3-way independent review
  extra repository research
  formal validation
  etc.

cost:
  tokens
  wall time
  worker slots

observed value:
  changed decision?
  found real defect?
  prevented rework?
  caused unnecessary churn?
  produced reusable rule/sense?
```

Then Projector can gradually discover things like:

> “Architecture decisions touching shared contracts benefit enormously from one independent challenger.”

but:

> “Three independent reviews of ordinary adapters almost never change the result.”

Or:

> “This analyzer family is now mature enough that deterministic checks outperform model review.”

That is exactly the sort of **continuous evidence-based self-tuning** I think fits Projector.

I’d add one constraint, though: don't let self-tuning itself become another hungry pet. 🐉 The meta-optimizer needs the same economics. Prefer accumulated passive telemetry and occasional bounded recalibration over continuously spending models deciding how many models to spend.

So yes, when you read my previous answer, interpret every suggested mechanism through this stronger invariant:

> **Projector should optimize marginal intelligence per unit cost, across the whole development trajectory, not maximize intelligence applied to every individual step.**

In fact, I'd make that a first-class architectural objective rather than merely an implementation optimization.
