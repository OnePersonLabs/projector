## 1. The strongest thesis is not “put the project in weights”

It is:

> **Project-specific understanding should be accumulated and computationally available across development acts, rather than repeatedly reconstructed by whichever generalist happens to be making the next change.**

The full loop matters:

```text
Provenance-bearing experience
           ↓
Current conception + useful historical rationale
           ↓
Cleanly rebuilt, learned project representation
           ↕
Frontier agent + developer discrimination
           ↓
Implementation changes
           ↓
Independent verification + new evidence
```

Your distinction between the current conception and useful historical experience is essential. Without it, the learned model could be an efficiently compressed record of yesterday’s confusion. The attached curriculum proposal preserves that distinction explicitly. fileciteturn1file2

But there is a further distinction worth protecting:

**The counterpart must perform project-specific reasoning, not merely return project-specific context.**

A weak counterpart says:

> “Here are relevant passages about identity, sessions, and assessment.”

A strong counterpart says:

> “Your proposal changes which entity owns this evidence. That breaks a deduplication assumption elsewhere, which affects persistent assessment. The existing exception for offline feedback does not authorize that ownership change.”

In the second case, the frontier agent does not have to reconstruct the entire conception to benefit from it. Some of the reasoning has already happened in the counterpart.

**That is how the two-model architecture could remain useful despite communicating through words.** The communication channel can be textual without text being the only persistent computational representation.

The potential advantage is therefore partly **amortized understanding**, and partly **division of reasoning responsibility**. Not just compression.

## 2. Three assumptions we should stop treating as conclusions

### Neural representation does not automatically mean holistic representation

We have not established that prose is fundamentally incapable of carrying S, or that weights preserve its relationships more faithfully.

There is a relevant experimental warning: **Cartridges** found that simply training a compressed neural representation to predict the source corpus produced memorization without the desired generality. Their self-study and context-distillation procedure mattered substantially. citeturn604957view0

So the useful distinction is not:

> Words fragment. Neural states integrate.

It is:

> **Which representation and learning procedure preserve the right consequences under the workloads we care about?**

That is a harder claim, but also one we can investigate.

### Clean rebuilding gives recoverability, not guaranteed correctness

Your event-sourced rebuild idea removes dependence on the previous project model’s accumulated training trajectory. That is valuable.

It does not remove inadequate learning, interference during the fresh training run, missing examples, or mistakes in the reconciled curriculum. Nor does a rebuild automatically reproduce identical weights across software and hardware environments. PyTorch’s reproducibility guidance explicitly warns about those limits. citeturn742902view0

We should preserve exact accepted model artifacts for rollback and separately test whether fresh builds recover equivalent behavior.

### The curriculum compiler can become the new slop generator

This is the most important internal threat.

If it misunderstands a distinction, invents a rationale, or “helpfully” removes an intentional exception, the trained counterpart may become extremely consistent around the wrong conception.

Then the frontier model agrees with it, the generated code agrees with both, and we have manufactured **agreement without correctness**.

An immutable event log lets us recover the original evidence. It does not make the interpretation of that evidence sound.

Therefore the architecture needs an independent distinction between **what was accepted, what was observed, what was inferred, and what remains unresolved**. The model must not be allowed to make those categories interchangeable.

## 4. Your falsification threshold should become the experiment’s organizing principle

I would call the target a **minimum discriminating system**. That is an experimental criterion, not another Projector subsystem.

It must contain enough interacting structure that competent approaches can differ meaningfully in their ability to preserve it.

A starting design might involve **30–80 meaningful concepts and 50–200 substantive changes**, with overlapping constraints, scoped exceptions, rejected alternatives, and changes to foundational assumptions.

Those numbers are proposals, not magic thresholds. The important variables are:

**How many constraints jointly govern a change? How far apart is their evidence? How many exceptions qualify them? How much history has changed their meaning?**

A large repository of independent CRUD features could be an easier test than a much smaller system with deeply coupled authority, timing, identity, and persistence rules.

### A suitable world would preserve the difficult interactions

An instrumented music-platform environment could include persistent skill, sessions, assessment evidence, multiplayer authority, offline operation, device identity, audio clocks, privacy, plugins, and migrations.

That would **not** be a reduced specification for Psychord. It would be a controlled environment for testing the same kinds of conceptual interactions.

Consider this change:

> “Allow an offline guest session to join an existing multiplayer room without interrupting playback.”

Under the hypothetical world’s rules, that could require reconciling identity, avoiding duplicate evidence, preserving provisional feedback, maintaining assessment authority, translating clock domains, and respecting privacy boundaries.

Every local patch could look reasonable while the combined result violates the system.

**That is the territory where the proposal deserves to be tested.**

Operational infrastructure can be simplified with simulated services and clocks. The conceptual obligations cannot. A simulation would test the ownership and timing model, not substantiate production audio latency.

There is independent support for moving beyond isolated tasks: **SWE-EVO** evaluates coordinated, multi-file software evolution and reports a substantial performance gap from single-issue work for the systems it tested. ## 5. The most revealing tests would exercise judgment, not recall

### Same present state, different reasons

Create two project worlds that currently use the same audio backend.

In one, the choice was driven by a deployment constraint. In the other, it was driven by a scheduling property.

Change the deployment constraint.

A model that memorized the selected backend may give identical recommendations. A model that retained the decision structure should distinguish the worlds.

This tests your **why** directly.

All approaches receive the rationale evidence. The later discriminating scenario is held out.

### A distant consequence without being told where to look

Present an ordinary feature request without naming the relevant architectural rules.

Does the system discover the consequences that the request does not explicitly mention?

This matters because a system that succeeds only after being handed the correct conceptual slice has not demonstrated the benefit you are seeking.

### A legitimate reversal, not simply preservation

The system must sometimes stop preserving an old architectural decision.

Change a premise and test whether it revises the appropriate conclusion while retaining unrelated constraints.

Otherwise, an “anti-drift” model can become a sophisticated obstacle to legitimate evolution.

### Historical relevance that changes with context

Your Michal → Michael example offers a particularly useful variant.

Ordinarily, the typo history is irrelevant. But suppose external clients still invoke the original public identifier. Now that history carries a compatibility obligation.

The lesson is not “keep renames” or “discard renames.”

It is:

> **Historical relevance depends on present and possible consequences, not merely the event’s category.**

That is precisely the kind of discrimination a useful curriculum compiler must make.

### Reconstruction after substantial evolution

After many changes, remove a meaningful subsystem and reconstruct it.

Evaluate whether it recovers the current conception, including exceptions and newly revised assumptions. Do not merely test whether it recreates something plausible.

The removed source must genuinely be inaccessible through retrieval, caches, or tools. Otherwise we could accidentally benchmark source recovery rather than semantic reconstruction.

## 6. The baselines must be capable of beating us

This is where the experiment could most easily deceive us.

Suppose we carefully reconcile history, extract rationale, generate excellent examples, train the local model, and compare it with an agent reading disorganized Markdown.

A win would establish very little about neural representation.

The essential comparisons are:

| Comparison | What it isolates |
|---|---|
| Strong code/spec-driven development with history-aware, structured retrieval | Whether the whole system improves on competent existing practice |
| The **same reconciled curriculum**, used through explicit structures and retrieval | Whether better semantic curation explains the benefit |
| The same local counterpart model and tool surface, but **without project training** | Whether a second reasoning agent explains the benefit |
| The project-trained counterpart, otherwise matched | Whether learned project state adds material value |

Retrieval should not be represented by a deliberately weak top-k chunk search. Work such as **HippoRAG 2** explicitly addresses interconnected, associative knowledge through structured retrieval. That belongs in the comparison space. citeturn181925academia1

Everyone should have access to equivalent accepted evidence and developer judgments. We should measure both frontier-token consumption and total amortized cost, including curriculum construction, training, local inference, and human correction.

**The neural claim becomes interesting only if it survives the same-knowledge, strong-counterpart comparison.**

That does not mean the integrated system is worthless otherwise. It means we correctly identify what actually produced the improvement.

## 7. The decisive result is a degradation curve, not an impressive answer

The experiment should run continuous branches, each accumulating its own changes and mistakes.

At checkpoints, measure how far the implementation has moved from the **current accepted conception**, not from its initial architecture.

The central result would be something like:

> At comparable budgets, as conceptual coupling and change history increase, the learned-counterpart system accumulates materially fewer consequential violations and requires less corrective intervention.

We should also measure completed valid changes. A system that rejects everything must not win by introducing no defects.

Some paired clean-checkpoint trials would help diagnose individual failures. But they cannot substitute for the continuous trajectories, because those trajectories contain the compounding effect we care about.

Evaluation must sit outside the model’s self-description. Use executable checks where possible, structural checks for boundaries and ownership, controlled counterfactuals, and blinded adjudication for judgments that resist automation.

**Do not let the same model invent the curriculum, generate the test answers, and certify that it understood them.**

And hold out *combinations of principles and classes of changes*, not just different wording of familiar questions.

### What would actually weaken the hypothesis?

If learned state adds nothing over the same curriculum with structured retrieval, neural storage is not earning its place in that tested regime.

If it answers conceptual questions well but does not improve the evolving codebase, the collaboration or implementation process is the bottleneck.

If it works on snapshots but becomes stale or uneconomical under change, the lifecycle is the bottleneck.

If strong baselines remain coherent throughout the practical complexity range, that also matters. We should not keep adding artificial difficulty until we manufacture the preferred result.

None of these outcomes universally disproves every possible S-model. They can, however, decisively redirect **this implementation and this investment**.

## 8. What I would actually commit to building

**The complete causal loop, on a genuinely coupled evaluation world, using the least exotic infrastructure available.**

That means the experiment includes semantic reconciliation, a genuinely learned project state, frontier collaboration, material developer questions, clean rebuilding after revisions, and actual code evolution.

It does not require a new foundation model, a production-ready Projector integration, or the model-native runtime vision yet.

For “train from scratch,” I would initially mean **fresh project-specific learned state against a pinned pretrained base**, not pretraining a language model from zero.

I would also avoid making Projector itself the sole initial benchmark. Its conception is precisely what we are worried may already be disputed or distorted. It should become an important real-world validation target, but not the only source of both training material and the definition of correctness.

The prototype should be crude in packaging and serious in semantics.

**Preserve the interacting mechanism. Cut deployment polish, not conceptual load.**
