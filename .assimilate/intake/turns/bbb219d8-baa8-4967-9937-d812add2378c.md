## 3. Parallelism needs two green lights

Your earlier throughput handoff emphasized keeping ready independent work fronts occupied. I would explicitly revise that policy: **independence makes parallel execution eligible for consideration; it does not make it worthwhile.** fileciteturn2file2

Every split needs both:

**Semantic independence:** Can these workers proceed without independently settling the same unresolved contract, ownership question, or shared assumption?

**Economic benefit:** Will the combined result arrive sooner, or be materially better, after setup, duplicate context, review, integration, contention, and repair are included?

A worktree separates checkouts. It does not establish semantic independence or eliminate shared-resource problems. Codex’s documentation also notes that worktrees carry their own files, dependencies, and build caches. citeturn971102view6

For example, in a hypothetical Projector change:

> Two analyzer implementations behind an accepted, stable analyzer contract could be good parallel candidates.

But:

> A hashing change and an invalidation change that are still discovering their shared meaning should probably stay with one strong owner, even when their files do not overlap.

The scheduler should also recognize that these are different choices:

**Delegation ≠ parallelism ≠ worktree creation ≠ changing models.**

A sequential specialist can be useful. A read-only investigation might need no worktree. Parallel test processes might need no additional model. A small task might deserve none of the above.

The target becomes **integrated, accepted progress**, not a screen full of busy agents.

## 5. No automatic rationing of competence

The normal policy has a simple invariant:

> **Same task, same evidence, same available capabilities, same preferences: same recommendation, regardless of remaining allowance.**
