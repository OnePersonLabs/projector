# Quality and work economics

The governing preference is cost-conscious work at the right level of competence. Choose the model, reasoning effort, context, work boundaries, delegation, execution topology, and verification together so that the result has justified quality at efficient total cost. Avoid lower settings reasonably expected to produce sloppier work or greater repair burden, and avoid higher settings whose marginal benefit does not justify their cost.

This is fully compatible with rejecting automatic quality downgrades when quota runs low. Remaining allowance does not change what competence the work needs. Scarcity control is, at most, an explicitly enabled manual option. When adequate execution becomes unavailable, preserve useful state and stop safely unless an already-qualified alternative can meet the same obligations. Spending the last allowance on defects that require later cleanup is false economy.

Model allocations, numerical scores, learned routers, and schedulers remain candidates with unproven economics.

## Compare complete routes to accepted outcomes

Cost per turn or per worker is an inadequate unit. The meaningful comparison is a correct, complete, integrated change, including its attributable later repair. A route's bill includes owner and worker execution, context preparation, duplicated investigation, consultation, verification, worktree setup, integration, owner corrections, and downstream liabilities. Human correction is work too. If the owner substantially rewrites a delegate's patch, charge that repair to the delegated route.

Quality includes behavioral correctness, requirement coverage, architectural coherence, maintainability, failure handling, and trustworthy evidence. Passing easy tests cannot compensate for a material deficit elsewhere. Mandatory acceptance obligations constrain candidate routes; the selection mechanism cannot weaken them to make a cheap route appear adequate.

Tokens, subscription consumption, separate allowances, API spend, local resources, integrated completion time, and human effort are distinct quantities. Label estimates and retain uncertainty. Avoid double-counting child usage included in parent totals or observed repairs as future liability. A separate allowance does not make poor execution economical.

An additional investigation earns its place when the likely improvement in a consequential decision, avoided repair, or reusable understanding warrants its full cost. Timing matters: cheap evidence should narrow the question before expensive scrutiny, and scrutiny should arrive before a mistaken assumption spreads through dependent work. An exact numerical return is unnecessary when the inputs do not support one.

Reject work whose expected value does not justify its burden; distinguish that from useful work whose right time has not arrived. A meaningful deferral keeps the condition that would make reconsideration worthwhile, such as a stabilized interface or repeated demand. Recording that condition does not itself authorize a background monitor or create an obligation to keep checking it.

## Keep ownership where understanding is coupled

A strong owner should directly handle work whose implementation continues to discover architectural meaning. Splitting every difficult question into a consultation can lose the accumulated understanding needed to recognize the next problem. The owner must remain an implementer and integrator, not a ceremonial manager who repeatedly reconstructs delegated work.

Useful boundaries follow shared assumptions, contracts, and debugging state. Once those settle, a bounded implementation may require less judgment. The strongest saving is often removing uncertainty from the task before selecting less expensive execution. A short task can still be consequential; a large mechanical task can be well constrained.

A delegation packet should carry observable objectives, relevant source and decision references, negative constraints, permitted decisions and effects, unresolved questions, a valid state basis, and an independent acceptance path. Preparing and checking it must cost less than completing the work with the already-informed owner. Summaries are aids to navigation; evidence remains available behind them.

Refresh stale context from current project state when it improves reliability, preserving obligations and decisions. Neither routine resets nor endless retention is inherently efficient.

Model and effort are separate choices. More reasoning on one model is not interchangeable with another model's competence, and extra reasoning cannot supply inaccessible facts. Named Astra, Sol, Luna, Terra, and Spark allocations are illustrative proposals from supplied material, not verified current recommendations or entitlements. Qualification belongs to a task, verifier, backend, and supported configuration together.

## Delegation, concurrency, and worktrees are separate decisions

Every split needs both semantic admissibility and economic usefulness. Disjoint filenames do not establish independence when two changes interpret the same evolving contract. Shared services, databases, generated outputs, manifests, and integration resources also matter. A worktree separates checkouts; it does not settle meaning or isolate every effect.

Choices include owner-only execution, sequential specialists, independent investigation, parallel deterministic processes, and isolated writers. Choosing a model implies none of these. Agent slots need not be filled.

Parallel completion includes setup, constrained execution, contention, integration queues, reconciliation, validation, and repair. Measure the combined accepted result, not when children report completion. Require enough benefit to withstand uncertainty in overhead estimates. An informed owner finishing a small task locally can be the best strategy.

Integration needs deliberate responsibility: stop candidate writes, check actual effects and input validity, compose the combined revision, and verify affected obligations. A changed shared contract invalidates dependent conclusions even when Git merges cleanly. Cancellation, descendant control, supported model settings, and external-effect boundaries must come from actual host capabilities where required. A launch request or a model's self-description does not establish which model ran.

## Spend verification where it changes confidence

Choose execution and verification as one route. Deterministic obligations deserve deterministic checks. Semantic omissions and architectural mistakes may require independent scrutiny grounded in requirements and actual artifacts. Candidate-generated tests cannot exclusively define correctness; accepted intent, independent fixtures, observed behavior, and newly generated expectations have different evidentiary roles.

Independent challenge can be valuable for a consequential unresolved decision without becoming a standing committee. An objection should identify the relevant obligation and concrete evidence; advisory concerns should not acquire veto power merely by sounding serious. A focused falsifiable disagreement deserves a discriminating check, followed by bounded reconsideration. Routine work does not need a fixed panel, unanimous votes, or repeated global review.

Escalation should address the failure class. Missing evidence calls for retrieval, an unavailable environment for restoring a precondition, a clear local defect for focused repair, a contract dispute for owner judgment, and an inadequate verifier for better evidence. Repeated edits without new evidence call for a different diagnostic approach. Increasing effort after an arbitrary retry count is not a diagnosis.

## Learn economically without making optimization the project

Simple policy with passive outcome evidence is a credible starting point. Record the task basis, actual execution configuration, context and coordination costs, acceptance findings, corrections, and later linked defects. Compare matched task families; cheap routes assigned only easy work create misleading averages. Uncertain attribution remains uncertain, and unknown future repair is not zero.

The decisive baseline is a competent single owner given the same improved context and verification. Additional routes must earn their place in representative comparisons, including negative cases and integration failures. Reconsider qualifications when relevant models, tools, contracts, or assumptions change.

Repeated discoveries may become reusable selectors, contracts, fixtures, or deterministic transforms, reducing future demand for expensive reasoning. Promotion needs independent evidence and a validity scope so that a mistaken repair does not become an efficient rule for repeating the mistake. Reuse is valuable only when its maintenance and invalidation costs also pay.

Open choices include how much telemetry is worthwhile, where static policy suffices, when measured results justify adaptive routing, and which coherent units benefit from specialization. A dedicated economics engine is not required by the preference. The manual scarcity lever, if ever built, can restrict new work or visibly defer optional scope while preserving accepted quality; its detailed behavior remains a design choice. No quota threshold should silently lower competence, relax verification, or authorize a new billing route.
