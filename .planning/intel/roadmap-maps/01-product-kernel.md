## Success Criteria Contributions

### Phase 01 — Slice 0 Foundation/correctness substrate

- Deleting derived SQLite state and rebuilding from committed inputs reproduces accepted semantic state and deterministic indexes.
- Public schemas and cross-package references resolve, while bounded canonical edits avoid loading or rewriting the full graph.
- Dependency-scoped bindings rebind across unrelated root changes, stale on newly matching query results, and refuse absence proofs from non-closed lanes.
- Package dependency tests, semantic-hash fixtures, manifest-order fixtures, and crash recovery fixtures pass deterministically.

### Phase 02 — Slice 1 misplaced-script loop

- The misplaced-script fixture produces stable Projection Units and rejects misleading path proximity.
- Repeated precedent remains descriptive until an inspectable authority basis activates blocking governance.
- A state-bound deterministic repair moves the script, updates references, validates the result, reconciles, and emits a certificate.

### Phase 03 — Slice 2 signatures/invalidation/backdating

- Exact or validated semantic signatures suppress downstream work only when their declared assurance policy permits it.
- Heuristic similarity never proves validity, and rebuild evidence remains distinct from independent and historical/metamorphic evidence.
- Relevant signature/query changes invalidate scoped dependents while unrelated canonical changes permit lightweight rebinding.

### Phase 04 — Slice 3 behavioral intent/identity/relevance

- Synonymous requests reuse existing Concept, Requirement, or Scenario identities rather than creating duplicates.
- Relevance Closure includes governing cross-cutting semantics and known event/contract consumers while excluding unrelated domains.
- Behavioral Markdown or Gherkin is traceably derived from canonical Requirements and Scenarios, never treated as a second authority source.
- Missing relationships discovered during implementation become Planning Surprises and proposed reusable relations.

### Phase 05 — Slice 4 governance robustness/representations

- Human, compact-agent, behavior, and machine projections preserve protected semantic dimensions with explicit assurance and fallback.
- Profile changes invalidate only dependent projections, and compact output is rejected when measured total cost is net negative.
- Governance cycles terminate under declared fixed-point limits and emit the specified visible failures when they do not converge.
- The blocking mechanical subset of `human-technical@1` passes while style checks remain distinct from semantic proof.

### Phase 06 — Slice 5 progressive architecture

- Change preflight surfaces only material concerns and classifies each as `blocking-now`, `material-soon`, or `deferable`.
- Accepted decisions expose concern, option, authority, consequences, and typed Governance Basis; preferences remain nonbinding.
- A cross-platform expansion reuses unaffected decisions, refreshes only materially stale evidence, and presents a concise decision frontier.
- Decision validity is scope-specific and typed reconsideration triggers prevent unrelated scopes from being invalidated.

### Phase 08 — Slice 7 coverage/completion/cleanup

- Audit and completion reports expose modeled boundaries, known affected sets, possible frontiers, unavailable lanes, and unknowns.
- `projector complete --scope ... --budget ...` can stop and resume without losing accepted knowledge or cleanup state.
- Partial-coverage audit remains actionable and supports scoped, JSON, since, and severity-threshold behavior.

### Phase 09 — Slice 8 full change compiler/executor

- Change requests preserve relevance-before-impact ordering from intent analysis through reconciliation and certification.
- Plans, packets, approvals, and capsules bind exact dependencies, recover from interruption, and require applicable independent evidence.
- Direct edits resolve to one documented reconciliation class, and reverse-impact comparison reports predicted versus observed effects.
- Validation, rollback, operation evidence, compact receipts, and truthful completion are inspectable for every governed mutation.

### Phase 11 — Slice 10 modernization

- Architecture upgrades begin with measured repository friction or platform constraints and remain scoped.
- Modernization proposals pass through the same relevance, decision, impact, migration, and validation machinery as ordinary changes.

### Phase 12 — Slice 11 watch/CI/hardening

- All init variants are idempotent and honor offline, audit-only, deep, interactive, and autonomous boundaries.
- CI rejects missing or unresolved public schemas, inconsistent exports, undeclared projections, and API/schema version defects.
- Repeated initialization and CI execution produce stable results under recovery and hostile-input tests.

### Phase 13 — Slice 12 external surfaces

- The external adapter framework completes all sixteen required semantic subsystems without weakening local-kernel boundaries.
- External observations are revisioned and expose truthful observability, capability, snapshot, drift, and unavailable/open-world semantics.
- At least one high-value provider and generic HTTP/JSON pass independent adapter contract and reconciliation tests.

### Phase 14 — dogfooding/public release/GSD-to-Projector handoff

- Repeated real changes demonstrate the complete causal loop and make later equivalent work measurably cheaper through accepted reusable machinery.
- Release conformance verifies all fifteen product invariants and preserves canonical terminology across user-visible workflows.
- `projector init` and `projector explain` exercise the completed subsystem set with bounded context and inspectable authority/evidence traces.
- The GSD-to-Projector handoff runs on Projector's own repository and produces a reconciled durable certificate suitable for public release.
