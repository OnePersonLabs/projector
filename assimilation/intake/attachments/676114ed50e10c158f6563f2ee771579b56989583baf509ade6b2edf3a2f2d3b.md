# Projector: Persistent System Conception

**Cumulative design proposal, research specification, and evidence-gated build plan**

> **Recovery note, 2026-09-11:** This filename and artifact were explicitly created earlier in this chat, but the original sandbox bytes are no longer mounted in the active runtime. This document is a faithful reconstruction from the surrounding conversation and surviving project handoff material. It is intended to restore the missing synthesis source, not to claim byte-for-byte identity with the lost artifact.

## Status and scope

This document is a **research and architecture proposal**, not an accepted replacement for Projector's current authoritative implementation specification.

It records a cumulative hypothesis that emerged from discussion of AI software-development degradation, context-window limits, specification drift, semantic integrity, repo-local learning, event-sourced reconstruction, and the nature of concepts.

The strongest recommendation is:

> **Build the complete learning-and-development loop around a genuinely coupled, evolving system. Test whether persistent learned conception improves its evolution, rather than treating a toy demonstration or a renamed retrieval system as evidence.**

The proposal deliberately separates:

- desired capabilities and user intent;
- diagnoses that remain hypotheses;
- candidate mechanisms;
- evidence requirements;
- long-horizon possibilities that should not burden the first experiment.

The goal is not to make a neural mechanism win. The goal is to discover whether software development becomes materially more coherent when project-specific understanding is accumulated and computationally available across development acts instead of repeatedly reconstructed from fragments.

---

# 1. Problem statement

Current agentic software development appears to suffer from a deeper failure than imperfect prompting, weak memory retrieval, insufficient tests, or bad planning decomposition.

A software project contains more than its source code. It also contains an evolving conception of:

- what the system is;
- why it exists in its present form;
- what distinctions matter;
- what owns what;
- which tradeoffs were deliberate;
- which alternatives were rejected and under what assumptions;
- what must remain true;
- what may vary freely;
- what should happen when foundational assumptions change;
- which implementation details are essential and which are disposable realizations.

Call this evolving conception **S**.

A useful shorthand is:

\[
S = \{\text{intent, rationale, constraints, architecture, semantics, ownership, tradeoffs, history, non-goals, relations, viable transformations}\}
\]

This is not asserted to be a complete ontology. It is a handle for the thing current development methods continually try to reconstruct.

The suspected failure loop is:

\[
S \rightarrow \text{artifacts} \rightarrow \text{partial context} \rightarrow \hat S_t \rightarrow \text{change} \rightarrow \text{new artifacts}
\]

Every coding agent receives only a bounded projection of the project and must infer a temporary approximation \(\hat S_t\). Even excellent retrieval, specs, planning files, architectural documents, tests, and memory systems typically improve which fragments enter the context window. They do not necessarily remove the repeated reconstruction step.

Over many changes, this creates a plausible mechanism for semantic entropy:

- the agent reconstructs a partial conception;
- it makes locally reasonable changes;
- some project meaning remains only implicit or is distorted;
- the next agent reconstructs from the altered artifact field;
- the process repeats.

This is a hypothesis, not an established measurement of Projector or agentic development as a whole. The proposal exists to make it testable.

---

# 2. The central inversion: meaning is primary; artifacts are projections

The deepest design shift is:

> **Do not assume prose, source code, diagrams, tests, or architecture documents are the canonical form of the project conception. Treat them as projections, observations, constraints, or evidence about a deeper project-specific organization.**

This distinction should be kept explicit:

\[
\boxed{\text{Concept} \neq \text{Representation} \neq \text{Projection} \neq \text{Evidence}}
\]

A sentence such as:

> Skill belongs to the player, not to games.

is not itself the entire concept. Its meaning can imply consequences for persistence, assessment, ownership, APIs, multiplayer behavior, analytics, migration, tests, and product behavior.

A mature S should support those consequences even when they were not all enumerated in one paragraph.

## 2.1 The NeRF analogy

The NeRF analogy is intentionally loose but useful.

A neural radiance field does not primarily store a pile of rendered images. It represents something from which views can be rendered under different camera conditions.

Analogously, S should support many task-conditioned expressions:

\[
R(S,q,v) \rightarrow \text{projection}
\]

where \(q\) is a question or task and \(v\) is a viewpoint, role, or context.

Possible projections include:

- an explanation for a new developer;
- an architecture decision;
- a task-local agent context;
- a change-impact analysis;
- a diagram;
- a test suite;
- a code implementation;
- a migration plan;
- a counterexample;
- a critique of a proposed refactor.

The analogy is not a claim that software meaning is literally a radiance field. Its purpose is to reject the assumption that the rendered view must be the thing being preserved.

## 2.2 Concepts as dynamic organization

The strongest formulation reached in discussion was approximately:

> **A concept is a dynamically organized action-pattern: a field of mutually constituting relationships, meaningful differences, intensities, tendencies, and possible transformations that possesses sufficient integrity to maintain a characteristic identity across its variations and expressions.**

For Projector, the practical implication is more important than the metaphysics:

> A project conception should preserve not only current facts but the meaningful relationships, rationale, and identity-preserving transformations that let the system change without accidentally becoming a different system.

This warns against making a list of nouns and edges the unquestioned primitive. Explicit identities and graphs may remain extremely useful for provenance, accountability, querying, and exact constraints, while the learned conception can preserve richer overlapping organization.

# 3. The why is load-bearing

S must robustly contain the **why**, not only the current answer.

A decision represented only as:

> Use backend B.

is too weak if future correctness depends on knowing:

> Use backend B because of assumptions X and Y, to preserve qualities P and Q; reconsider when X or Y changes.

The desired learned competence is closer to a project-specific decision function:

\[
D = (\text{choice},\text{because},\text{under assumptions},\text{reconsider when})
\]

This matters because future work often changes the premises while leaving the old conclusion visible in code or documentation.

A system that remembers the conclusion but not its causal basis may preserve obsolete architecture with perfect consistency.

# 4. Developer hardening through discriminating dialogue

S should not be treated as something the developer fully authors in polished prose.

The proposal is to harden it through **strategically chosen iterative discrimination**.

The agent or Projector counterpart should identify questions whose answers materially separate different architectural worlds.

Example:

> A game observes timing improvement that appears only inside its own mechanic. What is the intended relationship to canonical skill?
>
> A. The game updates canonical skill directly.  
> B. The game emits evidence and an assessment system decides.  
> C. The game owns a game-specific projection that may later affect canonical skill.  
> D. None of these / nuance.

The objective is not generic clarification. It is maximum reduction of **consequential ambiguity**.

A useful selection principle is:

> **What unresolved question, if interpreted incorrectly, would produce the largest downstream architectural divergence?**

The developer's answer becomes provenance-bearing evidence. It should not merely vanish into chat history or depend on a model remembering that a conversation occurred.

## 4.1 Developer recognition can precede articulation

The developer may be able to recognize that a transformation lost something before being able to formalize exactly what was lost.

That is valuable evidence.

A useful interaction pattern is:

1. propose competing interpretations or transformations;
2. let the developer identify the one that feels wrong, incomplete, or over-narrow;
3. articulate the missing distinction;
4. generate a counterexample or discriminating scenario;
5. obtain confirmation before promoting the interpretation into accepted project evidence.

This supports a richer loop:

\[
\text{recognition} \rightarrow \text{articulation} \rightarrow \text{discrimination} \rightarrow \text{accepted consequence}
\]

## 4.2 Semantic stiffness

A project region is weakly specified when small interpretive changes produce materially different decisions.

One proposed evaluation notion is **semantic stiffness**: low decision variance across independent reconstructions of the same accepted evidence.

High disagreement does not prove the developer must answer a question. It is a useful trigger for investigation.

# 5. The repo-specific neural counterpart

The core candidate mechanism is a **repository-associated learned project model** that collaborates with a frontier agent.

The relationship is:

```text
                 DEVELOPER
                     ^
                     |
          intent / judgment / correction
                     |
          +----------+----------+
          |                     |
   FRONTIER AGENT          PROJECT MODEL
   Codex / Claude              S-model
          |                     |
 general reasoning       project-specific
 implementation          integrated conception
 invention               rationale / continuity
          |                     |
          +----------+----------+
                     |
              Projector protocol
                     |
      repository / tests / runtime / evidence
```

The frontier model supplies broad programming ability and high-capability reasoning.

The S-model's narrower job is:

> **Know this project extraordinarily well and reason from its accumulated conception.**

The strong version is not a RAG service that returns relevant passages.

A weak counterpart says:

> Here are documents about identity, sessions, and assessment.

A strong counterpart says:

> Your proposal moves evidence authority into the session. That conflicts with the persistent assessment model and changes deduplication behavior. The existing offline exception does not authorize that ownership shift.

In the second case, the frontier model does not need to reconstruct every relationship itself. The counterpart is performing project-specific reasoning.

This means the benefit can exist even when the inter-model protocol is textual. The gain is not merely token compression. It is **amortized project understanding and division of reasoning responsibility**.

## 5.1 Candidate interaction surface

The exact interface is replaceable. It might be a CLI, MCP tool, hook, skill, or direct local service.

Illustrative operations:

```text
projector orient <task>
projector ask <question>
projector challenge <proposal>
projector impact <change>
projector explain <concept-or-decision>
projector validate <diff>
projector uncertainty
projector interrogate
```

The important contract is that the frontier agent understands it is collaborating with a project-specific counterpart, not consulting an unquestionable oracle.

## 5.2 Repo-native lifecycle

The model should be associated with a specific project revision and be reproducible from durable project evidence.

A conceptual repo layout might contain:

```text
.projector/
    events/
    evidence/
    curriculum/
    model-manifest/
    anchors/
    lineage/
```

Large weights or adapters need not live in ordinary Git. They can be content-addressed and pinned by manifest. The semantic requirement is revision binding, provenance, rollback, and rebuildability.

Branches may eventually represent divergent project conceptions as well as divergent code. Merging then becomes semantic reconciliation of accepted evidence and decisions rather than weight averaging.

---

# 6. Do not merely train a little LLM on the repository

A naive approach would fine-tune a small model on source files and documentation and call the result the project conception.

That risks reproducing the original problem in compressed form.

The learning target should not be rote reproduction of serializations. It should reward project-specific judgment and derived consequences.

The question is not:

> Can the model quote the project's architecture?

It is:

> Can it correctly infer consequences, detect distortions, revise conclusions when assumptions change, and generate coherent project-specific decisions under unfamiliar combinations?

Neural representation does not automatically provide holistic understanding. That remains an empirical question.

---

# 7. Event-sourced clean rebuilding

A major correction to the initial continual-learning concern is to avoid treating the current trained model as canonical.

Instead:

\[
E_{\le t} \xrightarrow{\text{reconcile / compile / train}} S_t
\]

where the durable event/evidence history is preserved and the learned project state is a derived artifact.

This permits a clean conceptual rule:

> **Never depend on the previous model as the only carrier of project truth. Preserve provenance-bearing experience, then reconstruct understanding.**

The trained S-model becomes disposable.

Benefits include:

- rollback;
- historical reconstruction;
- branch-specific conception;
- recovery from bad incremental adaptation;
- reprocessing old evidence with a better curriculum compiler;
- comparing independently rebuilt project models.

A strict caveat is necessary:

> Event sourcing preserves what happened and what was recorded. It does not magically turn every recorded interpretation into truth.

The system must keep accepted developer intent, observed facts, inferred interpretations, model suggestions, rejected alternatives, and unresolved ambiguity distinguishable.

# 8. The semantic curriculum compiler

Raw chronological replay is not the desired learning material.

The user supplied the key distinction:

- A typo such as `Michal -> Michael` is normally not useful history for rebuilding project understanding.
- Replacing FMOD with another audio system, and the reasons for doing so, can remain highly valuable because the old alternative may recur under similar assumptions.

Therefore derive two major classes of training information:

## 8.1 State compression

**What is true now?**

Remove superseded incidental states, transient scaffolding, accidental names, and intermediate implementations that no longer carry semantic consequences.

## 8.2 Experience compression

**What did history teach us that still improves future decisions?**

Preserve or generalize:

- reasons alternatives failed;
- conditional tradeoffs;
- surprising failure modes;
- assumptions behind decisions;
- counterexamples;
- conceptual distinctions exposed by mistakes;
- recurring architectural traps;
- conditions under which an old decision becomes relevant again.

The useful criterion for a superseded event \(e\) is approximately:

\[
V(e) = I(e;\text{future decisions}\mid C_{\text{current}})
\]

Informally:

> Given that the model already knows the current project state, does retaining this historical material materially improve future project-specific judgment?

The governing compression rule is:

> **Preserve causal information; discard incidental chronology.**

## 8.3 Generalize episodes into reusable lessons

Several backend failures need not remain several giant narratives if the durable lesson is:

> Audio engines are acceptable only when they satisfy timing, browser/WASM, ownership, and deployment constraints A/B/C.

The raw events remain for provenance. The curriculum can train primarily on the generalized lesson plus representative evidence.

## 8.4 Historical relevance is context-dependent

Even apparently trivial history can become important.

`Michal -> Michael` is normally discardable. If an external client still calls the original public identifier, that rename now carries a compatibility obligation.

Therefore the curriculum compiler cannot classify history using a simple permanent taxonomy such as "renames are noise".

Historical relevance depends on present and possible consequences.

## 8.5 The curriculum itself must not become a new lossy source of truth

The curriculum compiler is an internal threat.

If it silently invents a rationale, removes an intentional exception, or merges distinctions that matter, the resulting S-model may become extremely coherent around the wrong conception.

Therefore:

- preserve the raw evidence;
- retain provenance from derived lessons back to evidence;
- distinguish accepted facts from inferred compressions;
- allow clean reprocessing;
- evaluate the curriculum independently of the model trained from it.

The curriculum should not necessarily be one enormous canonical prose specification. It should increasingly resemble a **learning environment** containing facts, transformations, comparisons, counterexamples, before/after episodes, rationales, and project-specific discriminations.

# 9. Conceptual learning target

A higher-fidelity S-model should learn more than static facts.

It should learn:

- relationships and ownership;
- meaningful distinctions;
- causal rationale;
- tolerated degrees of freedom;
- identity-preserving transformations;
- boundary conditions;
- conditional preferences;
- counterfactual consequences;
- known unresolved ambiguity;
- how a concept manifests differently in code, tests, UX, infrastructure, and explanation.

A concept should not be reduced to a single similarity vector and then assumed complete. Distributed vectors may be useful internal coordinates, but the learning procedure must support structured consequences, negation, conditionality, authority, provenance, and transformations.

The practical test is behavioral:

> Does the learned state preserve the right distinctions under change?

---

# 10. Partial projections and multi-view consistency

A project conception may render into prose, code, diagrams, tests, examples, and other representations.

A tempting objective is:

\[
E(R_i(S)) \approx S
\]

for every view \(i\).

That is too strong because many legitimate views are intentionally incomplete.

A UI mockup should not be forced to contain a persistence policy. A concise explanation should not hallucinate architectural details that were absent from the view.

A better requirement is:

> **Recover what the view actually expresses, preserve uncertainty about what it does not express, and require consistency where views overlap.**

Then perform stronger intervention tests:

- change one authority relation in S;
- predict which interfaces, tests, behaviors, explanations, or implementation boundaries must change;
- verify that unrelated properties remain stable.

Round-trip consistency alone is insufficient because cooperating encoders/decoders can learn shortcuts that reconstruct without preserving the intended semantics.

---

# 11. Semantic reconstructability: the discardable-code criterion

The user's strongest long-horizon criterion is:

> **Could the implementation be thrown away and regenerated from S while retaining the material identity of this project?**

The criterion is not textual equality.

Given S and unavoidable external constraints \(E\):

\[
I' = \operatorname{Render}(S,E)
\]

then evaluate whether:

\[
\operatorname{Semantics}(I') \approx \operatorname{Semantics}(I)
\]

Material semantics can include:

- behavior;
- ownership;
- architecture;
- intentional exceptions;
- security and privacy obligations;
- important performance properties;
- UX/product semantics;
- migration obligations;
- rejected alternatives whose rationale still matters.

Implementation details that are genuine degrees of freedom need not reproduce identically.

This yields a powerful evaluation ladder:

```text
remove a function
remove a module
remove a package
remove a subsystem
remove most or all implementation
```

Then reconstruct from S plus allowed external information.

This directly measures how much project-specific information lives in the maintained conception rather than only in source code.

The criterion should not be interpreted as permission to delete production code before reconstruction has been demonstrated.

# 12. Code as a projection and the model-native software horizon

If semantic reconstructability becomes strong enough, code can be understood as one execution-oriented projection of project conception.

This leads to a spectrum:

| Level | Canonical emphasis | Role of code |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | source code | primary system representation |
| 1 | S + code | implementation generated/repaired from S |
| 2 | S + generated components | code increasingly becomes cached materialization |
| 3 | S + specialized models + tools | deterministic code where advantageous |
| 4 | persistent executable model | code becomes largely ephemeral actuation glue |

The far end is speculative, but clarifies the architecture:

\[
\text{Program} = \text{persistent conception} + \text{tools} + \text{constraints} + \text{rendered execution artifacts}
\]

Code exists because current computing substrates reward deterministic forms for:

- low latency;
- low marginal inference cost;
- formal analysis;
- reproducibility;
- transactions;
- security boundaries;
- hardware mapping;
- browser/platform APIs;
- real-time control.

This suggests a **fluid / crystalline** distinction:

### Fluid semantic layer

Good for contextual interpretation, adaptation, ambiguous decisions, personalization, evolving interaction, and semantic reasoning.

### Crystalline execution layer

Good for stable protocols, hot loops, schemas, real-time audio, rendering kernels, transactions, precise resource use, and reproducible computation.

Repeated stable model behavior could eventually be crystallized into deterministic artifacts, then invalidated and regenerated when assumptions change.

This is a long-horizon possibility. It must not become a prerequisite for the first S-model experiment.

# 13. What the first experiment must actually test

A toy feature or isolated concept is not an adequate falsification threshold for a thesis whose advantage is supposed to emerge from:

- interacting concepts;
- distant consequences;
- conflicting constraints;
- accumulated history;
- scoped exceptions;
- changing assumptions;
- context pressure.

The experimental target should be a **minimum discriminating system**: the smallest environment that still preserves the failure mode.

The exact size is not sacred. Illustrative discussion suggested approximately 30 to 80 meaningful concepts and 50 to 200 substantive changes, but conceptual coupling matters more than raw counts.

A smaller but tightly coupled system can be harder than a larger CRUD repository whose features barely interact.

## 13.1 Example evaluation world

A controlled music-platform environment could include:

- persistent player skill;
- assessment evidence;
- sessions;
- offline operation;
- multiplayer authority;
- device identity;
- audio clocks;
- privacy;
- plugins;
- migrations.

A change such as:

> Allow an offline guest session to join an existing multiplayer room without interrupting playback.

could implicate identity, evidence deduplication, provisional versus authoritative assessment, time-domain translation, privacy, and session lifetime.

Every local patch could be reasonable while the combined system becomes conceptually wrong.

Operational infrastructure may be simulated where doing so preserves the conceptual obligations under test. A simulated timing model cannot establish real production audio latency, but it can test ownership and translation semantics.

---

# 14. Strong baselines are mandatory

The experiment must not compare a carefully curated learned counterpart against a straw-man agent reading a pile of disorganized Markdown.

At minimum compare:

1. **Strong code/spec-driven development with history-aware structured retrieval.**
2. **The same reconciled curriculum/evidence through explicit structure and retrieval, without project training.**
3. **The same counterpart/tool surface using an untrained or generic local model.**
4. **The project-trained counterpart.**

All conditions should have access to equivalent accepted developer evidence where possible.

This isolates different possible sources of benefit:

- better curation;
- better retrieval;
- having a second reasoning agent;
- learned project-specific state;
- the full integrated lifecycle.

If the same curated knowledge with strong retrieval performs equally well, the neural representation has not earned its additional complexity in that regime.

That would still be a useful result.

---

# 15. The most discriminating evaluations

## 15.1 Same present state, different reasons

Construct two worlds with the same current implementation choice but different rationale.

Example:

- World A chose backend B due to a deployment restriction.
- World B chose backend B due to a scheduling property.

Then remove the deployment restriction.

A model that memorized the current backend may make the same recommendation in both worlds. A conception that retained the why should behave differently.

## 15.2 Distant consequences without hints

Give a normal feature request without naming the architectural concerns it implicates.

A successful system should discover important consequences that the request did not enumerate.

If it succeeds only after a human hands it the correct semantic slice, it has not demonstrated the desired holistic benefit.

## 15.3 Legitimate reversal

Change a foundational premise and test whether the system updates the appropriate decisions without needlessly destabilizing unrelated architecture.

An anti-drift system that merely freezes old conclusions is a sophisticated obstacle, not a solution.

## 15.4 Context-dependent historical relevance

Use an event that is normally irrelevant, such as a rename, then later introduce a compatibility dependency that makes it consequential.

This tests whether the system reasons about historical relevance rather than applying a static retention taxonomy.

## 15.5 Attractive but destructive refactors

Present changes that improve one dimension while erasing a meaningful distinction in another.

The target behavior is:

> This adds something useful, but loses something essential. Preserve the gain while restoring the lost invariant.

This mirrors a key conceptual correction in the discussion itself.

## 15.6 Reconstruction after sustained evolution

After many accepted changes, hide or delete a subsystem from the agent's accessible source and ask for reconstruction.

Prevent leakage through retrieval, caches, or hidden copies.

Evaluate architectural and behavioral equivalence, not textual similarity.

# 16. Evaluation must measure degradation curves

An impressive answer at one checkpoint is weak evidence.

The central experiment should follow **continuous development trajectories**.

At checkpoints measure divergence from the **current accepted conception**, not merely from the initial architecture.

The desired result would look like:

> At comparable budgets, as conceptual coupling and change history increase, the persistent-conception system accumulates fewer consequential semantic violations and requires less corrective intervention while still completing valid requested changes.

Measure at least:

- consequential invariant/ownership violations;
- missed cross-cutting impacts;
- valid changes completed;
- developer corrections required;
- new conceptual duplication or responsibility drift;
- false-positive blocking behavior;
- context tokens;
- local inference cost;
- training/rebuild cost;
- curriculum-generation cost;
- human intervention;
- total wall-clock or equivalent work cost.

A system that rejects all changes must not win merely because it introduces no defects.

---

# 17. Independent evidence and anti-self-confirmation

Do not let one model:

1. compile the curriculum;
2. generate the expected answers;
3. implement the change;
4. certify that it understood the project.

Where possible use independent evidence lanes:

- executable behavioral checks;
- structural ownership/boundary checks;
- held-out counterfactuals;
- controlled perturbations;
- independent model reviewers;
- blinded human adjudication for irreducibly semantic cases.

Hold out combinations of principles and classes of change, not only alternate wording of familiar examples.

Agreement between models is not truth. It is evidence to interpret.

---

# 18. Clean rebuilds, incremental caches, and reproducibility

Full project-specific reconstruction after every event may be unnecessarily expensive.

A practical lifecycle can separate canonical evidence from disposable accelerators:

```text
canonical events/evidence
        |
        +--> cheap incremental project-state cache
        |
        +--> periodic clean rebuild from accepted curriculum
```

Then compare the incremental state with the clean reconstruction.

If they materially diverge, discard or repair the incremental state.

This is analogous to incremental compilation versus a clean build: the cache may be valuable without becoming authoritative.

Fresh training is not guaranteed to reproduce identical weights across hardware/software stacks. Preserve accepted model artifacts and manifests for rollback, and evaluate behavioral equivalence of rebuilt states separately from byte identity.

---

# 19. What would weaken the hypothesis

The project should be designed to produce informative negative results.

The neural mechanism is weakened if:

- the same curriculum with strong structured retrieval performs equivalently;
- a generic second local model performs as well as a project-trained counterpart;
- learned state improves conceptual QA but not actual software evolution;
- clean rebuilding is too expensive or stale to be useful;
- the project model becomes confidently wrong because the curriculum compiler distorted evidence;
- strong baselines remain coherent throughout realistic complexity and budget ranges;
- the learned counterpart adds more operational burden than it removes.

These outcomes do not disprove every imaginable neural project representation. They would be sufficient reasons to redirect this implementation effort.

---

# 20. Prior-art posture

Do not claim that persistent neural project memory is itself wholly novel.

Nearby research and engineering directions include, among others:

- per-corpus learned or compressed context representations;
- adapter/LoRA generation from documents;
- neural or latent memory;
- structured associative retrieval and graph retrieval;
- continual learning and consolidation;
- long-horizon software-evolution benchmarks;
- vector-symbolic or hyperdimensional representations;
- latent model-to-model communication;
- self-adaptive software and semantic control architectures.

The potentially distinctive systems contribution is the combination of:

> **reconciled project experience + conditional rationale + cleanly reconstructible learned project state + active developer discrimination + frontier/local collaboration + demonstrated preservation of software coherence under sustained evolution.**

Novelty, scalability, and comparative advantage remain to be established.

---

# 21. Relationship to existing Projector ideas

The proposal should not automatically be implemented as another stack of Projector subsystems.

Projector already discusses or aspires to machinery such as concepts, semantic relations, authority, rationale, relevance/impact closure, invalidation, execution capsules, verification, reconciliation, and persistent governance.

Two possibilities must remain open:

1. a learned persistent conception **extends** those structures;
2. some existing structures are compensatory machinery for the missing persistent conception and could eventually be simplified or subsumed.

This is precisely why immediate redesign is dangerous.

For every proposed component, ask:

> **What irreducible information or capability does this preserve that would otherwise be lost?**

If two components preserve essentially the same thing, consider collapse.

If a component exists mostly to repair complexity introduced by another Projector abstraction, treat that as a warning.

If the learned project model fails to improve coherence beyond strong explicit approaches, do not preserve it merely because the architecture is elegant.

---

# 22. Build recommendation

## Build, but build the experiment rather than the mythology

The recommendation is **yes**, this direction deserves a serious implementation experiment.

But the first build should not attempt:

- a new foundation model;
- production-ready Projector integration;
- fully model-native applications;
- universal latent communication;
- one autonomous agent per concept;
- code deletion as an operational policy.

Instead build the complete causal loop at the smallest complexity that still preserves the claimed failure mode:

```text
provenance-bearing project experience
        ↓
semantic reconciliation / curriculum construction
        ↓
fresh project-specific learned state
        ↕
frontier agent collaboration
        ↕
strategic developer discrimination when needed
        ↓
actual software changes
        ↓
independent verification
        ↓
new evidence
        ↺
```

The prototype can be ugly in packaging.

It must be serious in semantics.

> **Preserve the interacting mechanism. Cut deployment polish, not conceptual load.**

# 23. Evidence-gated implementation checkpoints

The exact implementation technology is intentionally deferred, but the research program should cross gates rather than accrete features indefinitely.

## Gate 0: Evidence/curriculum pipeline

Demonstrate that the system can distinguish:

- accepted intent;
- observations;
- inferences;
- superseded states;
- useful rejected alternatives;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- incidental historical noise.

The raw evidence must remain recoverable.

## Gate 1: Project-specific learned judgment

Train fresh project-specific state against a pinned pretrained base or similarly controlled substrate.

Demonstrate held-out project-specific consequences that are not simple quotation or nearest-chunk retrieval.

If this cannot be shown, do not escalate to elaborate integration.

## Gate 2: Counterpart collaboration

Expose the learned state through a minimal interface and compare:

- generic frontier agent;
- strong retrieval/spec baseline;
- generic second-model counterpart;
- project-trained counterpart.

Measure quality and cost.

## Gate 3: Sustained evolution

Run continuous change trajectories over a genuinely coupled evaluation world.

Track degradation, corrections, valid completion, and total economics.

This is the main falsification threshold.

## Gate 4: Reconstruction

Remove increasingly large implementation regions and test semantic reconstruction without source leakage.

## Gate 5: Projector integration decision

Only after the preceding evidence should Projector decide whether the learned conception belongs:

- inside its core;
- as an optional plugin/service;
- as an experimental companion;
- or nowhere, if explicit approaches perform better.

# 24. Long-horizon research directions

These directions are intentionally deferred but should not be lost because they explain the architectural destination.

## 24.1 Learned project state as a persistent semantic substrate

The context window becomes a task-local viewport rather than the only place where integrated project understanding exists.

## 24.2 Direct latent collaboration

If future model interfaces permit embeddings, adapters, hidden states, or KV-level conditioning, the S-model could transmit project-specific conceptual activation more directly than through prose.

This should be tested, not assumed superior.

## 24.3 Specialized submodels

A project conception might eventually distill task-specific runtime or development models for regions such as UI, assessment, audio, planning, or domain semantics.

## 24.4 Adaptive crystallization

Stable semantic behavior may become deterministic code or other compiled artifacts; changed assumptions may invalidate and regenerate those artifacts.

## 24.5 Model-native software

At the speculative extreme, the persistent model becomes much of the program, using generated scripts and deterministic tools as actuators.

This possibility should inspire architecture without contaminating the near-term experiment with unnecessary ambition.

---

# 25. Non-negotiable distinctions to preserve

A later synthesis should not accidentally collapse the following:

1. **Persistent understanding is not the same thing as retrieval.**
2. **Textual communication between models does not imply text must be the persistent internal representation.**
3. **The why is not decorative documentation. It is part of project-specific judgment.**
4. **Event history is not automatically good training material.**
5. **Clean rebuilding improves recoverability, not guaranteed correctness.**
6. **The curriculum compiler can itself introduce semantic distortion.**
7. **Conceptual preservation must allow legitimate evolution.**
8. **A valid projection can be intentionally incomplete.**
9. **Model agreement is not an independent correctness oracle.**
10. **A toy concept does not test a claim about coupled long-horizon software evolution.**
11. **A neural mechanism must beat strong same-information alternatives to justify its additional cost.**
12. **Code-as-projection is a horizon and reconstructability criterion, not permission to discard source prematurely.**
13. **The architecture should be allowed to get simpler if persistent conception subsumes compensating machinery.**

---

# 26. Current cumulative position

The strongest surviving thesis is not:

> Put the repository into neural weights.

It is:

> **Project-specific understanding should be accumulated and computationally available across development acts, rather than repeatedly reconstructed by whichever generalist happens to be making the next change.**

The strongest candidate architecture is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{provenance-bearing experience}
\rightarrow
\text{semantic curriculum}
\rightarrow
\text{fresh project-specific learned conception}
\leftrightarrow
\text{frontier generalist}
\rightarrow
\text{software evolution}
\rightarrow
\text{new evidence}
}
\]

The central empirical question is:

> **Can this arrangement repeatedly evolve a genuinely coupled software system with materially less semantic degradation, less context reconstruction, or better total development economics than strong explicit/spec/retrieval baselines using equivalent evidence?**

If yes, Projector may have found a deeper foundation than another context-engineering layer.

If no, the experiment should tell us whether the valuable ingredient was instead the event model, the curriculum compiler, the developer-discrimination loop, better causal rationale, stronger evaluation, or some simpler explicit representation.

Either outcome advances the original objective: move downward toward the true causal layer of coherent software evolution rather than adding another compensating mechanism to the pile.

# 27. Handoff to future synthesis

Treat this document as a **cumulative research proposal and source of intent**, not a binding specification.

A future synthesis may:

- reject the neural mechanism;
- merge it with existing Projector machinery;
- discover a simpler representation that preserves the same benefit;
- separate near-term and long-term architecture differently;
- challenge the diagnosis of context reconstruction;
- replace the proposed curriculum or event model;
- conclude that explicit semantic structures outperform learned state.

What it should not do without confronting the evidence is silently reduce the proposal to:

> "Use better RAG and write better specs."

The distinctive claim under investigation is that a project may benefit from a **persistent computational conception that performs project-specific reasoning across development acts**, with prose, code, and other artifacts serving as evidence and projections rather than the sole place where that understanding exists.

The first serious proof is not an eloquent demo.

It is a degradation curve over sustained software evolution.
