## 1. The real bottleneck is not necessarily the communication alphabet

There are two substantially different architectures hiding inside “the local model explains something to the frontier model.”

**Architecture A: transfer the understanding.**

The local model produces a compressed description. The frontier model must reconstruct the relevant project conception, discover its consequences, and make the change.

**Architecture B: use the understanding where it already resides.**

The local counterpart identifies a consequential interaction, investigates it against current evidence, and returns a specific implication or construction. The frontier model handles the unresolved design or implementation work without independently reconstructing everything that produced that implication.

Your Persistent System Conception proposal and research checkpoint already make this distinction: **the proposed advantage is amortized project understanding and division of reasoning responsibility, not necessarily the elimination of language.** fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file10

Consider this illustrative exchange:

> “Making the session own assessment changes the meaning of reconnecting. Here is an execution trace in which the same observation is assessed twice.”

That can be vastly more useful than twenty pages explaining identity, sessions, assessment, and synchronization—even though both arrive through words.

The important difference is **what computation has already happened before the message arrives**.

But there is a limit. When a new design requires intricate joint reasoning that neither side can perform independently, communication can become the bottleneck again. “Let the local model remain the knower” is therefore a useful division of labor, not a universal escape hatch.

**The objective should be to minimize necessary reconstruction—not to minimize the presence of text.**

## 3. The missing bridge is an executable projection—not another explanation

The orbital sources contribute something important here.

They distinguish **knowing what should change semantically** from **deriving the concrete transformations that realize it**. They also separate precise application, impact coverage, and correct intention: success at one does not establish the others. fileciteturn0file2 fileciteturn0file6

My proposed connection is:

> **Let the frontier and local counterpart work on the same revision-bound candidate change, with different responsibilities, rather than repeatedly exchanging descriptions of separate imagined changes.**

Suppose we use the offline-guest example from your research sources. An illustrative interaction could be:

```text
prepare:
    goal: let an offline guest join an existing account
    source_basis: revision r91
    conception_basis: event revision e184

returns:
    candidate: p42
    predicted_conflict:
        identity reconciliation may change deduplication behavior

    supporting_source_query: q17
    proposed_counterexample: fixture k9
    candidate_repair: preserve observation identity across relinking

```

This is a sketch, not an existing API or a reported execution.

**The structured format is not the breakthrough.** As written, it is still mostly prose.

The breakthrough would be that:

- `q17` actually resolves and inspects the relevant current implementation.
- `k9` is an executable candidate counterexample whose result can contradict the prediction.
- `p42` is a shared candidate that the frontier model can inspect, complete, or challenge.

The local model’s understanding now produces something with **operational consequences outside either model’s explanation**.

The frontier might discover that the proposed repair is inadequate, implement a better one, and ask the counterpart to reassess the *actual diff*. Neither side needs to pretend that a fluent description is the changed system.

This also answers part of the NeRF intuition:

> **The shared object need not be a shared latent vector. It can be a shared, manipulable candidate whose different projections remain accountable to the same evidence.**

A prose explanation, a dependency query, a failing trace, a proposed patch, and an acceptance condition can all expose different aspects of that candidate.

### Why this is more than “agents talking with tools”

The protocol has to make three things real: **shared referents, actual execution, and state validity**.

The compiled-change handoff gives an excellent validity example: a plan discovers three consumers; a fourth appears later; the original three files remain unchanged. File hashes alone would not reveal that the plan’s coverage assumption is stale. Query membership matters. fileciteturn0file2

That is exactly the kind of thing the broker should handle mechanically. The neural counterpart can propose where to look; the broker should not accept remembered repository topology as current fact.

And the local model should not become a cheaper supreme architect. Your quality-frontier sources argue for retaining strong ownership of unresolved, tightly coupled judgment. The counterpart should make that owner’s job better bounded—not replace competence with project-specific confidence. fileciteturn0file9

## 4. The training material should teach the distinction you want preserved

Your “squashed concept-shaping prose history” idea still makes sense, with the distinction your other sources already developed:

**Squash incidental chronology. Preserve decision-relevant experience.**

The current accepted formulation matters. So does the episode in which an attractive “improvement” added something valuable while deleting something essential. Keeping only the final formulation can lose the discrimination that would prevent the next similar mistake. fileciteturn0file1

I would therefore make the basic learning unit a **discriminating change episode**, not a concept definition.

For example:

```text
Accepted purpose and relevant current commitments
+
Situation and proposed change
+
Two plausible interpretations or implementations
+
The consequence that distinguishes them
+
Evidence for that consequence
+
Accepted resolution, remaining uncertainty, and scope
```

That is a training-data shape, not a proposed new canonical document for every action.

The difficult part is generating these episodes faithfully. An assistant inventing plausible rationales and then training another model to repeat them would manufacture consistency, not understanding. Your sources explicitly identify the curriculum compiler as a possible new source of semantic corruption. fileciteturn0file5

### A concrete neural starting point

My starting candidate would be a capable pretrained local model with **fresh project-specific learned state**, trained on those episodes and their cross-concept combinations. A conventional adapter or learned KV representation would be a reasonable first implementation; nothing in the conceptual thesis requires inventing a new foundation architecture first.

There is relevant experimental support for taking the *training objective* seriously. **Cartridges** found that training a compact representation to reproduce its source corpus could produce memorization without the desired generality. Its self-study conversations and context-distillation objective materially changed that behavior. This supports the distinction between learning source text and learning how to use its information—not the stronger claim that it solves software conception. citeturn715913view0

I would keep exact current identifiers, source locations, and mechanically enforceable obligations accessible outside the weights. That does not reduce the neural model to retrieval. Its distinctive job remains **deriving relationships and consequences that were not already supplied as the answer**.

The useful division is:

**Learn judgment; resolve exact particulars; verify consequential claims.**

## 5. Clean rebuilding needs a present-tense companion

Your clean-rebuild mechanism solves an important lifecycle problem: the next project model need not inherit the previous adapter’s entire sequence of updates.

It does not guarantee that the new training run preserves every distinction. Your checkpoint already makes that correction explicitly. fileciteturn0file10

There is also a timing problem:

> What happens between accepting a meaningful project revision and finishing the next learned artifact?

I would keep the learned state bound to its training revision and supply a separate, explicit record of subsequent accepted changes. Current commitments must take precedence over superseded ones.

But **adding a recent-changes note is not proof that the old model has integrated the change**.

A small conceptual revision can invalidate many learned consequences. In those regions, the system should expose stale or uncertain judgment, obtain fresh reasoning, and rebuild when warranted. It should not treat “the new rule was present in the prompt” as a comprehension guarantee.

This suggests a practical separation:

**Durable evidence** records what was accepted, observed, proposed, or rejected.

**Learned conception** integrates that evidence into reusable judgment.

**Current-state machinery** binds each use of that judgment to the repository and semantic revision it actually concerns.

These are responsibilities. They do not require three new heavyweight Projector subsystems.

Your economics sources point in the same direction: deeper analysis and adaptation must earn their cost, including their own overhead. Rebuilding on every edit would be a policy choice to test, not a consequence of event sourcing. fileciteturn0file3

## 6. Direct neural communication is a real alternative—but a separate bet

I would not dismiss your intuition that something richer than prose could eventually cross the model boundary.

Two relevant research directions are concrete rather than merely metaphorical:

**Doc-to-LoRA** learns to turn context into a target model’s adapter, allowing subsequent queries without re-consuming that context. Its authors also report target-model-specific training requirements and a performance gap relative to in-context learning. It is evidence that context can become usable learned conditioning—not that arbitrary conceptual state can be transferred without distortion. citeturn715913view1

**XKV**, an August 2026 preprint, studies learned translation between the KV caches of heterogeneous language models, including models with different internal geometries. That is closer to the direct inter-model channel you are imagining. It still requires access to the participating models’ caches and a trained translator; it does not establish a universal latent interchange format or reliable software-conception transfer. citeturn683174view0

For a frontier endpoint exposing only ordinary message/tool interfaces, sending a list of latent coordinates as text is not equivalent to injecting those coordinates into its internal state.

So I would keep two paths distinct:

**Available-interface path:** persistent local judgment, shared executable artifacts, selective communication.

**Internals-access path:** learned conditioning or latent communication, evaluated for additional benefit.

Neither path makes fidelity automatic. But the first lets you investigate the core idea without making the second a prerequisite.

## 7. The earlier “test the interface first” recommendation needs one correction

Separating the interface experiment from the neural experiment is useful **for diagnosis**.

It should not become:

> “The generic-model version must win before the learned-model version is allowed to exist.”

That assumes the benefits are separable. They might not be.

A generic counterpart could be too expensive or unreliable to make interactive collaboration worthwhile, while a genuinely project-trained counterpart makes the same interface useful. Conversely, a good interface could explain most of the gain, with training adding little.

I would test that interaction directly:

| Local knowledge mechanism | Compiled-context interface | Shared intervention/change interface |
|---|---|---|
| Generic counterpart with strong retrieval and the reconciled evidence | A | B |
| Project-trained counterpart with equivalent evidence access | C | D |

Also retain a strong frontier-owner baseline without a second model. Otherwise we cannot tell whether the benefit came from simply adding another reasoner.

Most importantly, keep your **coupled, evolving system** requirement. One attractive response or one successful patch cannot test the claimed reduction in accumulated reconstruction and drift. Your sources are already emphatic about that, and correctly so. fileciteturn0file1 fileciteturn0file10

For your particular concern, I would vary the **communication budget** as well as the change horizon.

Does the trained, interactive arrangement preserve materially better decisions when it cannot send a small book to the frontier on every task? Does it still work when assumptions change? Does it discover distant consequences without being handed their names?

Charge for curriculum production, training, local inference, interaction, verification, and repair. Track valid changes completed alongside defects and false-positive objections. A model that “preserves the conception” by obstructing every change has failed.
