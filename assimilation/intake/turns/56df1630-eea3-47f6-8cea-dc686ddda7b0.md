## There's another subtle quota killer

**Don't make Sol discover architecture by thrashing through the filesystem.**

Suppose you have a big task.

### Strategy A

**Sol High**

It reads 40 files, forms hypothesis A, edits six files, runs tests, hypothesis fails, rereads, edits...

### Strategy B

**GPT-6 High**

It reads the architectural evidence, identifies the actual dependency structure, establishes the invariant, produces the change.

Then:

**Sol High**

implements the now-boring mechanical pieces.

Even though GPT-6's raw token rate is ~2.5× higher, Strategy B can easily win because **the expensive model compresses the search tree**.

That's the right metaphor:

> **Quota consumption ≈ breadth of search tree × price of exploring each node.**

A weaker model has cheaper nodes.

A stronger model may traverse **dramatically fewer nodes**.
