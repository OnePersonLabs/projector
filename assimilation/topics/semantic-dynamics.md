# Semantic dynamics

Semantic dynamics names a proposed learning target: understand how a project's meaningful organization responds to change. Persistent project understanding should recognize and generate viable transformations, preserve distinctions that still matter, and revise conclusions when their premises change. This extends [persistent project understanding](persistent-project-understanding.md); it does not replace durable evidence with an uninspectable theory of meaning. It is a conceptual direction and experimental hypothesis, not an implemented mathematical model.

The user describes a concept as a dynamic action-pattern of relationships with enough integrity to maintain a characteristic identity through variations and expressions. Subsequent refinement adds meaningful differences, intensities, tendencies, and possibilities while retaining action, relationality, transformation, and generativity. Those are perspectives on one organization. Turning each word into a mandatory component would lose the point of the formulation.

## Relationships, identity, and useful boundaries

Relationships can help constitute their participants. “Assessment authority” acquires meaning through its relation to evidence, people, sessions, and persistence. It is not necessarily a complete little object to which those relationships are later attached. Likewise, the relevance of stretching a triangle emerges through geometry, transformation, and the particular question; it need not be a property sealed inside a triangle container.

This permits overlapping concepts with useful but revisable boundaries. Timing integrity can cut across audio, UI, networking, and assessment. A stable reference gives people and tools a continuing subject for inquiry without asserting that it occupies an isolated region of a neural model or maps permanently to one module. Boundaries can move, concepts can split or merge, and old distinctions can cease to matter.

Graphs, vectors, hypergraphs, and other representations remain candidates. None is ruled out because relationships are constitutive; none establishes fidelity merely by looking more geometric. The requirement is operational: support meaningful conditional relations, differences, transformations, and uncertainty. Similarity alone is insufficient when nearly identical wording can conceal a change of authority.

The source metaphors of a field, gestalt, or conceptual “inside” motivate ways of thinking. They provide no empirical support for conscious concepts or direct mental communication. Their engineering value is the idea of a reusable perspective that activates relevant distinctions together. Reasoning from undo's perspective means understanding reversible consequences and interference, not giving an agent a first-person persona named Undo.

## What understanding should discriminate

Three changes can look alike in a diff but have different meanings. Renaming a session while preserving contracts may change only representation. Keeping every name while letting sessions certify canonical skill changes authority. Changing the premise that justified centralized assessment may authorize revising that authority while leaving unrelated obligations intact.

The target includes sensitivity and stability: determine what must move, what may move, and what should remain. Invariance and equivariance are useful candidate descriptions. Some changes should leave a relevant judgment unchanged; others should produce corresponding changes across implementation, tests, and explanations. No complete software transformation algebra has been established here, and a label such as equivariance does not supply one.

Consider a hypothetical collaborative editor. Undo should remove one person's edit without deleting another person's later contribution. An asynchronous render starts, the original edit is undone, another contribution arrives, and the render completes. Undo, collaboration, revision identity, rendering, and persistence jointly imply a consequence: the output may remain an artifact of its source revision but must not silently replace the current preview. Saving must not restore superseded state. This illustrates the desired inference across concepts; it is not a claim about an existing application.

The counterpart should derive such consequences without being handed every implicated concern. It should also discover coherent alternatives. If an optimization breaks revision identity, retaining a revision-bound output while separating its eligibility for current display may preserve the useful work. Exact source queries, traces, fixtures, and candidate patches then let both models test the interpretation against actual behavior.

## Integrity and development belong together

Integrity asks which distinctions a change must preserve. Development asks how the change can better express the project's purpose. These are compatible responsibilities. A music-learning product can preserve schemas and ownership boundaries while becoming increasingly interruptive and hostile to exploration. A useful project model should recognize that product loss and help invent a better interaction.

The same alternative can be preferable under one purpose and worse under another. Uninterrupted exploration may favor subtle feedback; deliberate assessment may favor explicit interruption. This requires conditional judgment about tradeoffs, not necessarily a universal numerical value score. Local simplification must also account for work or harm exported to other parts of the system.

Preservation cannot mean defending every old choice. Authorized evolution changes the accepted conception itself. The system needs to distinguish a new purpose, a refinement, an exception, a correction, and an exploratory possibility by their actual meaning and authority. These distinctions support development without requiring five new subsystems.

## Learning through discriminating changes

The most instructive episode can be an apparent improvement that gains something and loses something else. A reformulation that adds degrees and intensities but removes action and generativity demonstrates why “latest wording wins” is a poor reconciliation rule. The useful lesson is to integrate the gain while retaining the missing distinction.

A candidate learning episode therefore combines the relevant accepted purpose, a situation, plausible alternatives, the consequence that distinguishes them, supporting evidence, and the accepted resolution or remaining uncertainty. The curriculum can include cross-concept situations, counterfactuals, changed assumptions, and successful repairs. It should expose the organization through use rather than reduce all experience to definitions for memorization.

Perturbation-and-recovery training is another candidate: vary ownership, remove an exception, duplicate a responsibility, or offer a seductive refactor, then seek a coherent result satisfying the intended change. “Attractor” or “viability landscape” describes this hypothesis metaphorically until a measurable objective and effective mechanism exist. It does not select an energy-based neural architecture. Restoring the initial design regardless of changed intent would fail the objective.

## Projections and communication

Code, prose, diagrams, tests, interfaces, and explanations can express different aspects of the same conception. The loose NeRF analogy preserves this generative relationship between organization and views. It does not imply that every partial view reconstructs the entire organization.

A UI mockup may omit persistence policy. Multiple conceptions could produce that same view, so a faithful interpreter must retain uncertainty about what it cannot recover. The useful obligation is fidelity to expressed content and agreement where views overlap. A stronger test changes a meaningful relationship and checks appropriate consequences across views while preserving unrelated properties. Round-trip consistency alone can reward coordinated errors and cannot serve as independent authority.

Textual collaboration can put this understanding to work through shared, manipulable candidates and evidence. Direct learned conditioning or latent communication remains a further research path when participating model interfaces allow it. Those routes are compatible: the first tests whether persistent judgment improves decisions through available interactions; the second investigates whether a richer channel adds benefit. A textual list of latent coordinates is not by itself a functioning shared representation.

## The experimental implication

A credible experiment evolves a sufficiently coupled system through interacting changes and premise revisions. It tests novel useful designs, not only violation detection; legitimate reversals, not only memory; and consequences that were never explicitly supplied as answers. It should vary communication budget and change horizon, and compare strong code/spec/retrieval approaches with equivalent evidence and counterpart access.

Measure which consequences were missed or invented, how many valid changes were completed, whether meaningful distinctions survived, and the cost of repair and maintenance. Continuous trajectories reveal accumulating distortion; controlled checkpoints help identify its cause. Reconstruction after evolution tests whether material understanding survived outside the original source. Independent observations and adjudication must constrain evaluation.

The open scientific question is whether a learned organization can acquire enough integrated judgment to reduce repeated reconstruction while remaining corrigible and useful under change. Better terminology, a convincing explanation, or a toy concept cannot answer it. The promised capability is practical: recognize what a transformation gains and loses, derive its wider consequences, and help construct a better expression of the intended system.
