**5. Target ownership and dependencies**

The semantic layer owns accepted identities, facts, relationships, governance, and lineage. The change layer owns proposed semantic mutations and implementation targets. The implementation lifecycle owns candidate observation, execution, contribution accounting, verification, review, and publication. Derived indexes and representations have explicit source identities and authority limits.

One accepted semantic authority means one ownership model for facts, not one globally mutable state across all branches or one exclusive active worker. Independent worktrees can hold different versioned proposals and branch states. Bind observations, candidates, evidence, and recovery to their actual checkout and source revision. Reconcile them at the appropriate integration boundary.

Representation fidelity cannot establish that an agent received the representation. Delivery cannot establish that the agent followed it. Command success cannot establish the adequacy of assertions. Generated-byte identity cannot alone establish causation. These boundaries need connected evidence rather than shared success labels.

Avoid circular authority: observed code can supply evidence for a conceptual change but cannot silently author accepted meaning that is then used to certify the same code. A projection cannot approve its own source. A validator or reviewer cannot gain independence from a different label. Recovery state cannot override a later authorized source edit.

Reconcile current state before making design commitments. Establish authority and identity contracts before integrating targets and evidence. Establish candidate observation before recording current evidence. Reconcile evidence and contributions before final review. Publish only the exact reviewed state. Representation delivery and its storage repairs can proceed independently once their source and lifecycle bindings are stable. Provider work can proceed independently when its fact and completeness contracts are stable.
